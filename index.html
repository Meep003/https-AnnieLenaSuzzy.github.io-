02 / PREPARE

Start here

Understand the key terms, then work through a steady application plan.

Words to know

Stipend

A regular payment that helps cover living costs while you study. Check the amount, duration, and conditions.

Tuition waiver

A reduction or removal of tuition fees. It does not always cover other university fees or living costs.

Full package

For this guide, a full tuition waiver plus a monthly stipend that can cover basic living costs in that city.

Not stated

The official page we checked did not give the detail. Contact admissions or the scholarship office before you apply.

Apostille / legalization

An official process that confirms a document is genuine for use in another country. The process varies by country.

Expected-graduation letter

A letter from your university confirming that you are finishing your degree and when you expect to graduate.

Application roadmap

  1. 12–10 months beforeChoose programmes

    Check field fit, language, funding eligibility, and official deadlines.

  2. 10–8 months beforePlan tests and documents

    Check English tests. Request transcripts and ask referees early.

  3. 8–5 months beforeWrite and apply

    Prepare your study plan or statement. Submit scholarship forms separately if needed.

  4. After admissionConfirm and prepare

    Check award terms, document legalization, visa steps, and the start date.

BEFORE YOU APPLY

Build your document folder

  • Passport and ID
  • Transcripts and degree documents
  • Language-test record or exemption proof
  • CV and personal statement
  • Recommendation letters
  • Study or research plan, if required

DOCUMENT PREP

Translation & authentication tips

  • Check which document language the university accepts.
  • Ask whether translations must be certified and who may certify them.
  • Confirm whether notarisation, an apostille or legalization is required for your country.
  • Check delivery method and processing time before ordering documents.

07 / EVALUATE

Top 10 tier list

Four published criteria compare official funding, course fit, English-medium evidence, and application readiness.

01

How the score works

Financial support 40%

Tuition: 20 × the fraction of tuition covered (100% coverage = 20 points). Monthly cash: 10 × min(monthly NT$ amount ÷ NT$60,000, 1). Duration: 10 × funded months ÷ programme length (maximum 10 points). The cap is a score scale, not a living-cost claim. A recurring annual grant is divided by 12 only for score comparison; the profile keeps the annual total and does not imply a monthly payment schedule. One-time awards and unpriced in-kind benefits are not converted into monthly-cash points.

Course and field fit 25%

Field score: 10 for a dedicated strategic-communication degree, 8 for communication/media, 6 for adjacent communication, 4 for language/business communication, 2 for indirect fit, or 0 for no link. Add 5 points for each linked official relevant course (maximum 3). This rewards clear IBSC curriculum overlap.

English-medium evidence 10%

10 when an official source explicitly says the whole programme is taught in English; 5 for mixed or limited evidence; 0 when whole-programme English instruction is not confirmed. Language matters for IBSC applicants planning an English-taught degree.

Application readiness 25%

Five points each for a published programme deadline, scholarship deadline, fee terms, document-list completeness, and contact/start details. Document completeness is 5 × known checklist statuses ÷ 15; contact and start date are 2.5 each. Missing information earns points only in that score component.

Ranking rule: FULL PACKAGE means 100% tuition plus living support that covers basic costs; it always ranks above PARTLY FUNDED, regardless of points. Within each badge group, candidates sort by total score. Tiers are #1–3, #4–7, and #8–10.

NCCU IMICS cash points use the separate MOE Taiwan Scholarship (NT$20,000/month), not the programme pilot award (NT$10,000/month). Eligibility and application are separate; see the profile and linked MOE source.
